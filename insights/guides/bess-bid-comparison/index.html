The lowest $/kWh offer
can be the wrong comparison.

A reproducible example of how DC nameplate, net AC guarantees and excluded integration scope change a battery-storage bid comparison. Use it to organize supplier clarifications before ranking prices.

Published November 4, 2026 · Publisher: AGI&M Assets Inc. · All bidders, prices and guarantees below are hypothetical.

Download the blank bid worksheetEditorial method

One service, three different offer boundaries.

Assume the owner needs 10 MW and 40 MWh net AC at a named PCC meter at beginning of life. Three offers quote different battery capacities and include different scope. All illustrative costs are USD at the same hypothetical comparison date, before tax, financing and any unlisted costs. These are not market benchmarks or supplier quotations.

What the brochure denominator suggests

Quoted price divided by DC nameplate gives A: $144/kWh, B: $175/kWh and C: $160/kWh. On that narrow measure A appears cheapest. None of those ratios tells the owner whether the same AC service is included.

What the boundary-adjusted numerator suggests

Adding only the identified outside-scope allowances gives A: $9.00 million, B: $8.80 million and C: $8.60 million. This is a scoped comparison subtotal, not complete installed cost: unknown costs remain unknown rather than zero.

Do not normalize away a failed requirement.

OfferSubtotal / guaranteed net AC kWhScreening decision
ANot calculable: denominator missingRequest a net AC guarantee and scope evidence. Do not infer one from 50 MWh DC.
B$8,800,000 ÷ 40,000 = $220/kWh ACMatches the energy requirement on paper; verify power, conditions and remaining obligations.
C$8,600,000 ÷ 36,000 = $238.89/kWh ACDoes not meet 40 MWh. Obtain a compliant revised offer before ranking it.

Offer B is the only energy-comparable candidate in this example. It is not automatically the winner: the table contains no verified listing, site approval, delivery schedule, credit review, warranty or power-test evidence. Nor can C simply be multiplied by 40/36: battery quantity, PCS capability, land, fire layout and auxiliary loads do not necessarily scale together.

How much does the integration allowance matter?

A equals B's $8.80 million subtotal when A's outside-scope allowance is $1.60 million: $8.80 million − $7.20 million. Above $1.60 million A costs more on the listed scope; below it A costs less. This sensitivity identifies which estimate deserves clarification. It still cannot establish A's compliance while its AC guarantee is absent.

Use the worksheet as an evidence register.

The downloadable CSV is blank for real bidder values. It carries eight comparison lines, a required evidence field, a supplier document/revision field, a status field and a clarification owner. Open it in a spreadsheet or import it into an existing procurement register; no registration or email address is required.

First establish comparability

Record the service year, net meter boundary, MW/MWh duty, environmental conditions and usable SOC window. Ask for the exact battery/PCS configuration and model-specific safety evidence. Distinguish equipment supply, delivery, installation and commissioning.

Use statuses such as Documented, Not supplied, Exception or Requires clarification. “Documented” means a document exists; it is not independent verification of its claim.

Then record commercial exposure

List currency, exchange-rate basis if needed, delivery term, freight, duties, taxes, installation, utility works and warranty support consistently. Keep supplier prices separate from owner estimates and record estimate dates. Do not hide missing scope in an arbitrary percentage uplift.

Keep safety, grid compliance and service requirements as separate pass/fail gates. A weighted price score must not compensate for a missing essential requirement.

Beginning of life is not end of warranty

A guaranteed 40 MWh at commissioning does not prove 40 MWh at year ten. Compare the same contract year, duty and meter boundary. Record augmentation responsibility, throughput limits and maintenance exclusions before attempting a lifetime comparison. This example deliberately does not calculate investment return or levelized cost.

Source boundary and original contribution

The offer data, arithmetic, sensitivity and worksheet structure are AGI&M's original hypothetical examples. No supplier performance, current price or project delivery is claimed. The distinction between system certification and component evidence is grounded in UL Solutions' ESS testing and certification overview; an offer must supply its actual configuration-specific evidence.
