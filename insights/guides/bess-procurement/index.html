AGI&M / Equipment Engineering Reference

Specify the energy system.
Then evaluate the equipment.

Battery storage procurement requires more than a container capacity, a cell brand or a price per kilowatt-hour. Project teams need consistent definitions of delivered energy, power conversion, interconnection responsibility and the evidence behind each equipment claim.

Commercial and Technical Scope

Six boundaries that define a comparable offer.

An equipment specification becomes actionable when different bidders quote the same electrical boundary and acceptance requirements. The following considerations help structure an early request for quotation.

01 / ENERGY

DC nameplate versus AC delivered energy

DC energy capacity does not equal contractual energy at the point of connection. State depth of discharge, inverter and transformer losses, temperature conditions, auxiliaries, degradation and end-of-warranty guarantees separately. Identify the reference meter and the duration of the acceptance test.

02 / POWER

Continuous power versus short-duration limits

Separate PCS nameplate from sustained AC output under the specified ambient temperature, DC voltage and grid conditions. Define reactive-power capability, grid-forming or grid-following requirements and what happens when the BMS derates available power.

03 / INTERFACE

Equipment battery limits and contractor scope

Identify the actual supplier boundary: container, rack, PCS, step-up transformer, MV switchgear, cabling, auxiliary power and communication gateway. Each omitted interface creates commissioning, warranty or schedule risk if responsibilities are not assigned.

04 / STANDARDS

Certification evidence is model-specific

Request certificates and test reports for the offered equipment model and configuration. UL 9540, UL 9540A, NFPA 855 and utility interconnection requirements serve different purposes; citing a standard is not evidence that a particular product is listed, tested or accepted at the intended site.

05 / ACCEPTANCE

Factory and site test evidence

Confirm the FAT procedure and test witnesses, protection and controls interfaces, BMS/PCS alarms, communications-failure response, performance-test procedure and documentation package. SAT should address actual installed boundaries and utility commissioning requirements.

06 / OPERATIONS

Warranty, maintenance and operating envelope

Compare cycle and calendar limits, ambient-temperature ranges, auxiliary consumption, degradation conditions, spare parts, remote support, firmware policy and liability for unavailable capacity. A headline warranty term has little meaning without its exclusions and performance definition.

Worked Procurement Example / Reproducible Calculation

What does a 10 MW / four-hour AC delivery requirement actually imply?

The numbers below are a hypothetical engineering illustration, not an AGI&M project, supplier quotation, product rating or guaranteed design. A project engineer must replace every assumption with the actual meter boundary, operating envelope and vendor evidence before issuing an RFQ.

Input at the specified boundaryIllustrative valueWhat a real tender must establish
Net export required at the point of common coupling (PCC)10.0 MW for 4.0 hMinimum sustained net AC export and duration, including applicable temperature and voltage ranges
Auxiliary load during discharge0.20 MW, constantWhether HVAC and site auxiliaries are measured behind the export meter; measured operating profile
DC-to-AC discharge-path efficiency92% (0.92)Guaranteed efficiency at the relevant discharge rate, transformer boundary and operating conditions
Permitted usable fraction of DC nameplate90% (0.90)Usable state-of-charge window and BMS operating restrictions
End-of-warranty retained capacity fraction80% (0.80)Contractual capacity-retention schedule, test method and throughput/temperature exclusions

Step 1 — Define the actual service

At the PCC, net delivered energy is 10.0 MW × 4.0 h = 40.0 MWh AC. With a 0.20 MW behind-meter auxiliary demand, the discharge path must supply (10.0 + 0.20) × 4.0 = 40.8 MWh AC before auxiliary consumption.

Step 2 — Translate AC output to usable DC

Under the assumed 92% DC-to-AC efficiency, usable discharged battery energy is 40.8 ÷ 0.92 = 44.35 MWh DC. With a 90% usable nameplate fraction, an idealized beginning-of-life requirement is 44.35 ÷ 0.90 = 49.28 MWh DC nameplate.

Step 3 — Test the end-of-warranty guarantee

If the same four-hour net export must remain possible after the assumed 20% capacity loss, the illustrative installed beginning-of-life nameplate becomes 49.28 ÷ 0.80 = 61.59 MWh DC. This is not an equipment selection: power, current, thermal, SOC and calendar-life restrictions can make the result insufficient.

Step 4 — State what the estimate excludes

The example assumes constant load, constant conversion efficiency and a single discharge. It omits temperature and altitude derating, battery voltage and C-rate limits, reserve requirements, availability, charging losses, degradation shape, augmentation timing, grid-code constraints and project contingency. Test these separately rather than hiding them inside an unexplained multiplier.

Reproduce the example

Use this explicit relationship for the stated assumptions: installed DC nameplate (MWh) = [(net PCC MW + behind-meter auxiliary MW) × discharge hours] ÷ [DC-to-AC efficiency × usable DC fraction × retained-capacity fraction]. Substituting 10.0, 0.20, 4.0, 0.92, 0.90 and 0.80 gives approximately 61.6 MWh DC. If an RFQ guarantees only beginning-of-life output, remove the retained-capacity factor and specify that limitation explicitly.

Procurement Decision Tool / Bid Normalization

A bid comparison that exposes exclusions before the price comparison.

A defensible shortlist uses an identical reference duty and acceptance boundary for every bidder. Record the supplier document and revision beside each claim; mark absent evidence Not supplied rather than treating a brochure statement as a verified guarantee.

Evaluation lineComparable evidence required from every bidderDecision if missing

2. Battery and PCS configurationExact model/revision, quantity, DC nameplate, usable SOC, PCS continuous MW and one-line diagramCannot establish product or power equivalence
3. Energy guaranteeBeginning- and end-of-term AC energy, discharge profile, degradation, augmentation and auxiliariesTreat lifetime performance as unverified
4. Safety and listingSystem listing scope, UL 9540A test level/revision, configuration match and local authority requirementsDo not assume approval from cell-only evidence
5. Grid interfacePCS grid-code settings, models, protection studies, reactive capability and utility review responsibilitiesExclude interconnection acceptance from the claimed turnkey scope
6. Installation boundaryMV transformer, switchgear, cable, HVAC, fire detection, EMS gateway, foundations and responsibility matrixFlag missing scope as owner/EPC cost exposure
7. FAT, SAT and warrantyWitness points, measurable pass criteria, documentation, service response and warranty exclusionsWithhold evidence-based acceptance
8. Commercial normalizationIncoterm, taxes/duties, schedule, installation exclusions, warranties and total landed/integrated costDo not rank bids solely on quoted $/kWh

Pass/fail first

Before evaluating the lowest bid, require a matching AC service definition, identifiable certified/tested configuration, feasible interconnection path, complete responsibility assignment and measurable acceptance criteria. Document every exception in a dated clarification register.

Then compare cost and risk

Compare normalized installed cost and contractual AC energy at the same life stage and test boundary. Keep unresolved civil, utility, logistics and commissioning costs as explicit exclusions or owner contingencies; do not silently assign them a zero value.

Source and Methodology Notes

Standards references are not equipment approvals.

UL 9540 addresses the evaluated energy-storage system. UL 9540A is a fire-propagation test method, not a standalone blanket certification of every installation. NFPA 855 addresses installation safety; applicability depends on the adopted edition, jurisdiction and authority having jurisdiction. Demand the offered configuration's actual listing, reports, limitations and site-specific acceptance.

Authoritative referenceUse in an engineering review
UL Solutions — ESS testing and certificationDistinguish system-level certification scope from component and inverter standards
UL Solutions — UL 9540A test methodCheck tested level, relevant edition and offered equipment configuration
NFPA — Standard 855Identify installation rules and adopted-edition issues to confirm with the local authority

Capacity and Cost Logic

Translate load requirements into an equipment boundary.

Start with the operating objective

Peak shaving, backup power, energy arbitrage and renewable smoothing can place different demands on cycling frequency, state-of-charge reserve, inverter behavior and control architecture. An early BESS request should state the dominant duty and the constraints of the point of common coupling.

For constant-power operation, duration is delivered AC energy divided by sustained AC power. Nominal DC capacity must also accommodate usable depth of discharge and discharge-path efficiency. These first-pass calculations exclude the actual project deratings listed above.

Evaluate total project cost consistently

Equipment quotations are not equivalent to installed cost. Freight, duties, engineering, site works, transformer and switchgear integration, civil scope, testing, grid studies, interconnection upgrades and warranty support may change the comparison. Use the same delivery incoterm and responsibilities across bidders.

The goal is a traceable cost and performance comparison, not the lowest unqualified container price. A documented risk and exclusion register makes the commercial review more useful than a single $/kWh figure.
